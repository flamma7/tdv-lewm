TDV-LeWM: Temporal-Difference Supervision for Latent World-Model Planning

Applying TDV-style motion learning to LeWM on OGBench-Cube

Code  |  Model weights

I was inspired by the TDV paper’s simple motion objective — current embedding plus encoded pixel difference should recover the next embedding — to ask whether that bias would help planning in a latent world model. Adding TDV supervision to LeWM improves CEM, iCEM, and Adam planning on OGBench-Cube, with a larger gap once evaluation noops are removed. I kept LeWM’s SIGReg collapse regularizer instead of TDV’s DINO-style knowledge distillation, trained on RunPod Community Cloud with a controller that retries flaky GPUs, and found that the same pixel-difference bias does not further straighten the latent trajectory. In the sampling used for OGBench-Cube, including in stable-worldmodel, about 39% of scenarios are cube noops: the cube already starts within the goal threshold. Success rates drop for every world model after removing noop scenarios, particularly for optimizing the cube’s goal displacement. This highlights a significant amount of LeWM’s reported success on OGBenchCube is due to a measurement bias in the form of noop scenarios. TDV-LeWM’s success rate gap compared to LeWM widens after removing noops. All models prioritize the gripper over the cube when optimizing toward the goal latent. This is consistent with the gripper occupying more pixels and therefore more of the latent manifold that MPC optimizes.

Summary. I took LeWM’s architecture and applied a TDV-inspired loss to bias the latent space toward motion and pixel differences. This was motivated by TDV’s gains over other SSL methods in visually dense environments, which I hypothesized could aid LeWM’s performance in the visually complex task of OGBenchCube. In the process I discovered a large confound of no-op scenarios in the evaluation set that was inflating LeWM and likely many other world models’ performances. TDV-LeWM outperforms LeWM on optimizing the position of the cube, gripper and both objects, and its lead extends when removing no-op scenarios, supporting the hypothesis that biasing the latent space toward motion and pixel differences helps planning in visually dense environments.

Primary Contributions:
  • LeWM is augmented with a motion encoder and TDV-style loss during training to bias the latent space toward pixel differences. The performance of TDV-LeWM is evaluated via the success rates of various MPC planners (CEM, iCEM, Adam) on OGBench-Cube.
  • No-op confound analysis: A brief analysis demonstrates that a substantial fraction of no-operation scenarios in the OGBenchCube evaluation set (e.g., cases where the cube or gripper is already at the goal) artificially inflates MPC success metrics on the task.

Overview

$$(z_t, H_t) = \mathrm{Enc}(x_t), \qquad \hat{z}_{t+1} = \mathrm{Pred}(x_t, a_t)$$ $$\Delta z_t = m_\phi(\Delta x_t, H_t), \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TDV}} = \lVert z_t + \Delta z_t - z_{t+1} \rVert^2$$ $$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}} + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SIGReg}} + \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TDV}}$$
  • Architecture. I augment LeWM with a motion encoder and a TDV-style temporal prediction loss. The motion encoder \(m_\phi\) takes the pixel difference \(\Delta x_t\) and cross-attends to the current latent feature map \(H_t\), producing a latent displacement \(\Delta z_t\). This displacement is trained such that \(z_t+\Delta z_t\approx z_{t+1}\).
  • Collapse prevention. I kept LeWM’s SIGReg term and did not retrain TDV’s DINO-style teacher network with a stop-gradient (redundant with SIGReg regularization).
  • Motion encoder. \(\Delta x_t = x_{t+1} - x_t\) is patchified and fed to a ViT-T/14 motion encoder that cross-attends to the image encoder’s feature map \(H_t\). \(\Delta z_t\) is outputted from the CLS token. The TDV paper found that contextualizing pixel differences with the current latent was important.
  • Data. OGBench-Cube (single cube) through stable-worldmodel (galilai-group/ogb_cube_single). I kept the usual frameskip of 5 and scenario horizon of 25.
  • No-op Analysis. A no-op is a scenario whose start displacement is already below the 0.04 success threshold. In the OGBench-Cube sampling used by stable-worldmodel, about 38% of eval scenarios are cube no-ops (the object typically used for success rates), ~9% are gripper no-ops, and ~4% are both (union ~43%). These cases inflate reported success, especially cube and both-object metrics.
  • Training. ViT-T/14 encoder, 10 epochs, batch size 128, learning rate \(5\times 10^{-5}\), on RTX 5090s via RunPod. Best TDV-LeWM run: \(\alpha=1.0\), \(\lambda=0.25\) (tdv_a1.0_lam0.25_lr5e-5). \(\mathrm{LeWM_{REP}}\) uses \(\lambda=0.1\) (lewm_lam0.1_lr5e-5).
  • TDV-LeWM MPC Analysis. TDV-LeWM outperforms LeWM on every MPC planner (CEM, iCEM, and Adam); the gap is largest under CEM: both-object success 68.5% vs 66.4% / 63.6% for \(\mathrm{LeWM_{REP}}\) / \(\mathrm{LeWM_{PUB}}\), and 56.1% vs 50.8% / 48.4% with noops removed. TDV's benefit decreases with stronger planners such as iCEM.
  • TDV-LeWM Planning Analysis. This analysis measured whether latent costs rank physically better action sequences. TDV-LeWM gives the best ranking of cube outcomes and the combined cube–gripper objective, with pooled Spearman correlations of \(\rho_{\mathrm{cube}} = 0.608\) and \(\rho_{\mathrm{cg}} = 0.657\), and positive \(\rho_{\mathrm{cg}}\) on 94% of scenarios. Across all models, gripper outcomes are ranked more faithfully than cube outcomes.
  • Latent Straightening. Motivated by recent work on temporal straightening for planning in JEPA world models (Wang et al.), I analyzed whether TDV induces implicit temporal straightening of \(z_t\). TDV explicitly parameterizes the one-step latent displacement as \(z_{t+1}\approx z_t+\Delta z_t\), with the motion encoder directly learning the latent “velocity” \(z_{t+1}-z_t\). However, I find that TDV does not increase the paper’s straightness metric, \(C_t=\cos(z_{t+1}-z_t,\;z_{t+2}-z_{t+1})\), and therefore does not appear to reduce latent trajectory curvature.

Infrastructure

I used RunPod Community Cloud for training and evaluation sweeps. The controller had to deal with unreliable NVIDIA drivers (start the container, detect a bad machine, shut down, and redeploy), frequent checkpoints because jobs can die mid-run, and a single job-config YAML that specifies hardware, hyperparameters, evaluation type, and seeds.

OGBenchCube No-Operation Scenarios

A no-op is defined as a scenario whose start displacement is already below the 4cm success threshold for OGBenchCube in the Mujoco simulator. Thereby these scenarios are trivially successful for any planner. This occurs as a sampling artifact because the evaluation set samples random 25-step sequences from the dataset, and a large percentage of sequences have starting and ending object positions within the 4cm threshold. I include gripper no-ops because LeWM encodes the full scene into a single latent. As the next section shows, LeWM typically prioritizes minimizing gripper latent displacement over the cube's.

Does the latent cost rank real plans?

Higher positive Spearman \(\rho\) means the model assigns lower latent cost to action sequences that actually produce better physical outcomes, which is what MPC needs.

Latent Straightening

Motivated by recent work on temporal straightening for planning in JEPA world models (Wang et al.), I analyzed whether TDV induces implicit temporal straightening of \(z_t\). In the paper, Wang et al. define a latent trajectory as locally straight when consecutive velocities \(v_t = z_{t+1}-z_t\) and \(v_{t+1}=z_{t+2}-z_{t+1}\) point in similar directions as measured by their cosine similarity.

$$C_t = \cos(z_{t+1} - z_t,\, z_{t+2} - z_{t+1})$$

Larger values of \(C_t\) indicate that consecutive latent velocities are more aligned, corresponding to a locally straighter latent trajectory.

TDV explicitly parameterizes the one-step latent displacement as \(z_{t+1}\approx z_t+\Delta z_t\), with the motion encoder directly learning the latent “velocity” \(z_{t+1}-z_t\). This raisesed the question of whether TDV might implicitly encourage temporal straightening within the latent trajectory.

TDV-LeWM has a slightly lower mean consecutive-velocity cosine similarity (\(0.661\)) than LeWMREP (\(0.674\)) and LeWMPUB (\(0.669\)). TDV also places fewer transitions in the highest-alignment bin, \([0.80,1.00]\): 47.9% vs. 54.9% and 52.3% for LeWM\(_\mathrm{REP}\) and LeWM\(_\mathrm{PUB}\); however, it has more transitions instead falling into \([0.60,0.80)\). Together with the approximately unchanged mean straightness score, this suggests that the TDV augmentation turns out to not introduce any noticeable effect on straightening the latent space.

Future work

  1. TDV’s pixel-difference bias may help more in environments where actions do not fully explain visual transitions, such as Atari.
  2. stable-worldmodel includes appearance-robustness evaluation tools. Biasing the latent toward motion may help under color shifts, because the motion encoder sees \(\Delta x_t\) rather than absolute appearance.
  3. LeWM already performs well on the other main SWM environments (Two-Room, Reacher, and PushT), so there is less room for TDV to move the needle there.
